# Easy-Vibe Stage 1 Playbook: From Raw Clues to Validated Startup Concepts

This reference codifies the two foundational chapters of the Easy-Vibe curriculum:
1. **Where Creative Ideas Come From (`appendix-idea-sources`)** — 20-minute raw clue collection without premature judgment.
2. **How to Identify a Great Idea (`finding-great-idea`)** — Demand verification, need-tier classification, horizontal/vertical segmentation, and value reframing.

---

## Part 1: The 4-Step Community & Market Hunting Playbook (AI Engineer Edition)

When you do not yet have personal pain points or repeated peer requests, hunt systematically across four external channels. Never start from an AI model ("I want to use a Multi-Agent RAG") and look for a problem. Start from **manual friction** where humans are already spending time or money processing unstructured data.

### Step 1: Catch Manual Workflows on Reddit & Niche Forums (10 mins)
Users rarely ask for an AI tool by technical name; they complain about copy-pasting, formatting, or spending hours in spreadsheets.

**Search patterns:**
- `site:reddit.com "<niche>" ("is there a tool" OR "i spend hours" OR "manually")`
- `site:reddit.com "<niche>" ("tedious" OR "nightmare" OR "waste of time" OR "spreadsheet")`
- `site:reddit.com "<niche>" ("how do you automate" OR "copy paste" OR "every week")`

**Target non-tech subreddits:** `r/Freelancers`, `r/freelance`, `r/smallbusiness`, `r/Bookkeeping`, `r/Teachers`, `r/realestate`, `r/PropertyManagement`.

**What to record:** Exact verbatim quote, user persona/context, frequency, and direct post URL + date.

### Step 2: Mine 1-to-3 Star Reviews on Capterra & G2 (15 mins)
Instead of inventing a new category, inspect vertical software where businesses already pay monthly subscriptions but hate specific manual steps.

**Search patterns:**
- `site:capterra.com "<software or category>" ("Cons" OR "clunky" OR "manual" OR "frustrating")`
- `site:capterra.com "<software or category>" ("import" OR "formatting" OR "template" OR "too many clicks")`

**Why 2–3 stars beat 1 star:** 1-star reviews are often billing disputes or customer support rants. 2-to-3 star reviews come from active paying users who rely on the core workflow but suffer from missing conditional automation, broken mobile UX, or painful imports/exports.

### Step 3: Verify Solo-Founder Revenue & Launch Gaps on Indie Hackers & Product Hunt (10 mins)
- **Indie Hackers (`/products`)**: Look for solo products earning **$500–$5,000 MRR**. They reveal a narrow persona, a specific input format, and a repeatable acquisition channel.
- **Product Hunt**: Read the launch comments, not the marketing tagline. Questions like *"Can it handle [edge case X]?"* or *"Does it support [format Y]?"* expose immediate technical gaps.
- **Google Trends**: Compare how real users phrase the problem (`"AI meeting notes"` vs `"voice notes"`). Rising search interest shows attention shift, though not guaranteed willingness to pay.

### Step 4: Inspect Paid Repetitive Jobs on Upwork & Fiverr (10 mins)
Where clients repeatedly pay humans to process files or text, an AI workflow can deliver 10x speed at 1/10th the cost.

**Search patterns:**
- `site:upwork.com/freelance-jobs "<niche>" ("convert" OR "extract" OR "format" OR "transcribe")`
- `site:upwork.com/services "<niche>" ("clean" OR "spreadsheet" OR "PDF" OR "automation")`

## Part 2: The Xiaoming Trap & Need Hierarchy

In the Easy-Vibe case study, developer Xiaoming spent a full year and 200,000 RMB building a polished fitness app (courses, check-ins, community, analytics). Despite 50,000 downloads in month one, 7-day retention hit 5% and almost nobody paid:
1. **Existing solutions (Keep, FitTime) were already good enough** — switching costs were high.
2. **High behavioral friction** — users had to log workouts manually before receiving value.
3. **Abundant free alternatives** — generic features gave no reason to pay.

### The 3 Need Tiers (`pain` > `gratification` > `desire`)

| Tier | Driver | Analogy | Willingness to Pay | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pain (`pain`)** | Fear, financial loss, compliance risk, acute physical/time burden | Painkiller | **High** — leaving it unsolved has immediate consequences | Losing $2,300 to unbilled freelance scope creep; restaurant owner waking at 4am for wholesale market |
| **Gratification (`gratification`)** | Instant relief, speed, convenience | Fast reward | **Moderate** — strong retention hook, needs clear ROI for B2B/prosumer billing | One-click multi-format poster resizing; 30-minute delivery |
| **Desire (`desire`)** | Ideal self-image, discipline, aesthetics | Vitamin | **Low** — users procrastinate or use free tools | Logging daily water intake; generic workout habit tracker |

## Part 3: Reframing Ordinary Ideas into High-Value Concepts

1. **Horizontal Segmentation (Narrow the User Group)**:
   - Never target "all fitness users" or "all freelancers".
   - Split horizontally: *Postpartum mothers (0–6 months)* vs *Diabetics managing blood sugar* vs *Bodybuilders tracking macros*. Pick the segment with the highest acute pain and clearest budget.
2. **Vertical Context Timeline (Map the Exact Moment of Friction)**:
   - Trace the user's day hour-by-hour. What are their hands doing? What app is open? What emotion or fear triggers the action?
   - Example: A freelance web developer receives a "quick 5-minute tweak" email from a rude client 2 weeks after sign-off and fears damaging the relationship if they push back.
3. **Value Reframing Examples**:
   - *"Calorie photo counter"* $\rightarrow$ **Diabetic Pre-Meal Glycemic Safety Copilot**
   - *"Generic news summarizer"* $\rightarrow$ **Morning Investment Intelligence Officer for Analysts**
   - *"Campus second-hand marketplace"* $\rightarrow$ **Graduation 72-Hour Bulk Liquidation Concierge**
   - *"Freelance project manager"* $\rightarrow$ **Scope Creep Detector & Polite Change-Order Quote Generator**
